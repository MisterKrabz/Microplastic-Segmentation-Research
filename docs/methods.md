# Methods

## 2.1. Microplastic Detection and Instance Segmentation Pipeline

We developed a two-stage deep learning pipeline, termed MIRA (Microplastic Intelligent Recognition Architecture), for the automated detection and pixel-level segmentation of microplastic particles in darkfield optical microscopy images. Microplastic samples were collected from Lake Mendota (Madison, WI, USA), prepared on membrane filters, and imaged under 532 nm laser illumination. A total of 72 darkfield images were annotated with bounding boxes encompassing two morphological classes---aggregate and pellet---yielding 64 images for training and 8 images for validation. Annotations followed the YOLO format, with each image accompanied by a label file containing class indices and normalized bounding-box coordinates. No color-space augmentation (HSV) was applied during training, as the spectral contrast inherent to darkfield microscopy is informative for particle identification and must be preserved. Instead, microscopy-appropriate augmentations were employed: full 360-degree rotation (since samples lack a canonical orientation), horizontal and vertical flipping (each at 50% probability), mild scale jitter (&#177;25%), and slight translational shift (10%). Mosaic augmentation was disabled to preserve the spatial context of particles on the substrate.

## 2.2. Object Detection Model Training and Performance

The object detection stage utilized a YOLO11-Large architecture (25.3 M parameters, 86.6 GFLOPs) initialized from COCO-pretrained weights and fine-tuned on the microplastic dataset. The model was trained using the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1.5 &#215; 10&#8315;&#179;, cosine annealing to a final learning-rate fraction of 0.01, and a weight decay of 0.01. A 5-epoch linear warm-up was applied. Training was conducted at an input resolution of 1280 &#215; 1280 pixels with a batch size of 8 on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU (143 GB VRAM) provisioned through the Center for High Throughput Computing (CHTC) at the University of Wisconsin&#8211;Madison. Early stopping with a patience of 200 epochs halted training at epoch 977 (best weights saved at epoch 777), with the full run completing in approximately 0.57 hours of wall-clock time. On the held-out validation set (8 images, 123 annotated instances), the best model achieved an overall precision of 89.3%, recall of 96.1%, mAP@50 of 95.3%, and mAP@50-95 of 64.1%. Per-class performance was as follows: for the pellet class, precision reached 99.2% with an mAP@50 of 97.9%, while the aggregate class attained 97.2% recall and an mAP@50 of 92.8%. Inference speed was 4.3 ms per image, enabling near-real-time processing of large image batches. Agnostic non-maximum suppression (NMS) at an IoU threshold of 0.25 was applied during inference to eliminate duplicate overlapping detections regardless of predicted class.

## 2.3. Instance Segmentation and Chemical Identification

Each bounding box produced by the YOLO detection stage was passed as a spatial prompt to the Segment Anything Model 2 (SAM 2), a vision-foundation model pre-trained on large-scale segmentation data. SAM 2 was loaded from the Hiera-Large checkpoint (sam2.1_hiera_large.pt) with its default configuration. Prior to prompting, each bounding box was contracted inward by 10% of its width and height to remove background margin pixels that would otherwise degrade mask quality; coordinates were clamped to image bounds to prevent collapse. SAM 2 was run in single-mask mode for each box prompt independently: the image encoder processed the full image once, and the lightweight mask decoder was invoked per box, producing a logit map at full image resolution that was binarized at a threshold of 0.5 to obtain pixel-precise segmentation masks. When a CNN-predicted chemical map was available (stored as a comma-separated values file in which each cell contains a material label or "NA"), the pipeline additionally classified each segmented particle by mapping its binary mask onto the chemical grid---each grid cell uniformly covering a rectangular region of the image---and performing a majority vote among the non-background cells overlapped by the mask to assign a material identity (e.g., Polystyrene, Polymethyl Methacrylate, Polyethylene). The complete two-stage pipeline was packaged as the open-source Python framework *mira-microplastics*, providing programmatic access to YOLO-only detection, SAM 2-only segmentation, and the combined sequential workflow through a unified API.
